# Archaea Starter Knowledge Base

A concise, English-language foundation for learners entering archaeal research from microbiology, genomics, geochemistry, data science, or AI.

This is a starting map. Check current taxonomy, database releases, and primary literature before reusing any scientific claim.

## 1. Identity

Archaea are one of the major domains of cellular life. They are prokaryotic in cellular organization but evolutionarily distinct from Bacteria. Archaeal information-processing systems include components with deep relationships to eukaryotic transcription and replication.

**Common misconception:** not all archaea are extremophiles. Archaea occur in oceans, soils, sediments, host-associated systems, engineered environments, and many other non-extreme habitats.

## 2. Cell biology

- **Membranes:** ether-linked isoprenoid chains attached to glycerol-1-phosphate are characteristic. Some lineages form membrane-spanning tetraether lipids.
- **Cell envelopes:** many archaea have proteinaceous S-layers. They do not have bacterial peptidoglycan; some methanogens have pseudomurein.
- **Gene expression:** archaeal transcription has important similarities to eukaryotic systems.
- **Cell division:** mechanisms vary and include ESCRT-related and FtsZ-based systems.

## 3. Major metabolisms

### Methanogenesis

Biological methane production is restricted to Archaea. Pathways can use carbon dioxide, acetate, methylated compounds, and other substrates. Methyl-coenzyme M reductase is central to methanogenesis and related methane metabolism.

### Anaerobic oxidation of methane

Several archaeal groups oxidize methane without oxygen, often with microbial partners and geochemically coupled electron acceptors.

### Ammonia oxidation

Ammonia-oxidizing archaea are abundant in marine and terrestrial environments and can be important under low-ammonia conditions.

### Other strategies

Archaea use diverse carbon fixation pathways, heterotrophic metabolisms, sulfur and hydrogen transformations, and retinal-based light-driven ion pumping.

## 4. Ecology checklist

Before interpreting an environmental result:

1. Record habitat, depth, chemistry, sampling time, and collection method.
2. Distinguish relative abundance, absolute abundance, and activity.
3. Separate genomic potential from RNA, protein, metabolite, isotope, or rate evidence.
4. Consider partners, viruses, and spatial organization.
5. State whether evidence comes from isolates, enrichments, genomes, or communities.

## 5. Common evidence layers

| Layer | Useful for | Main caution |
|---|---|---|
| Amplicons | Targeted community composition | Primer bias and limited functional inference |
| Metagenomes | Community genes and reconstructed genomes | Assembly, binning, contamination, incompleteness |
| Metatranscriptomes | RNA expression at sampling time | Expression does not independently establish process rate |
| Proteomics | Detected proteins and pathway support | Extraction and reference-database dependence |
| Stable isotopes | Sources, transformations, and rates | Model assumptions and pathway overlap |
| Cultivation | Physiology under controlled conditions | Laboratory conditions may not represent the habitat |
| Microscopy | Structure, localization, and interactions | Identification and sampling limits |

## 6. AI in archaeal research

### Useful roles

- query expansion and literature triage;
- structured extraction from defined sources;
- annotation prioritization and anomaly review;
- code drafting and documentation;
- organization of competing hypotheses.

### High-risk failure modes

- invented citations or identifiers;
- outdated taxonomy;
- overconfident gene-function assignment;
- loss of environmental context;
- causal claims from correlations;
- conversion of genomic potential into claimed activity.

### Minimum evidence rule

No scientific claim should leave an AI-assisted workflow without:

1. a source identifier;
2. a supporting evidence sentence or data object;
3. an uncertainty note;
4. a named human verification step.

## 7. Starter glossary

- **AOA:** ammonia-oxidizing archaea.
- **ANME:** anaerobic methanotrophic archaea.
- **MAG:** metagenome-assembled genome.
- **mcrA:** a common marker for methyl-coenzyme M reductase.
- **S-layer:** a surface layer made from protein or glycoprotein subunits.
- **Syntrophy:** a metabolic partnership based on coupled reactions or exchanged products.
- **Tetraether lipid:** a membrane-spanning archaeal lipid found in several lineages and environments.
